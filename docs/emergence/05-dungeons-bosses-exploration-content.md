# Dungeons, World Bosses, Ruins, and Exploration

## Principle

> A dungeon is a persistent physical consequence of history, ecology, construction, conflict, or occupation.

Avoid generic procedural room graphs unless they have a causal origin.

## Dungeon sources

Abandoned infrastructure: mines, sewers, fortifications, bunkers, temples, laboratories, shipwrecks, underground stores.

Ecological transformation: monster lairs, fungal colonies, predator dens, flooded caves, overgrown ruins.

Historical catastrophe: battlefields, plague cities, collapsed settlements, disaster zones.

Social transformation: criminal hideouts, cult complexes, abandoned prisons, smuggler tunnels.

## Lifecycle

Dungeons can form, grow, change occupants, be looted, be reclaimed, collapse, become settlements, become sacred/tourist sites, or disappear. The same location can pass through multiple phases.

Track physical structure, occupants, ecology, resources, hazards, history, entrances, ownership/control, and recent activity.

## World bosses

A world boss is an emergent exceptional individual. Candidate triggers include genetic exceptionalism, age, accumulated traits, unusual environment, magical exposure, resource access, and unusual survival history.

The boss remains an ordinary simulation entity until properties cross an encounter threshold.

Boss territories produce prey depletion, migration, NPC fear, abandoned routes, damaged infrastructure, rumors, economic responses, and possibly cults. Killing a boss emits a major historical event and can alter ecology, population genetics, economy, beliefs, and future encounters. Do not use timer-based respawn as the source of exceptional individuals.

## Ruins

A ruin is the physical state of a formerly functional place. Creation causes must be stored: war, famine, disease, resource exhaustion, migration, disaster, or political collapse.

## History-derived POIs

High-importance events can leave persistent discoverable evidence. Candidate POIs depend on event importance, physical persistence, player relevance, and accessibility.

## Exploration

Exploration should ask “What happened here?” as well as “What is here?”. Discoveries can include evidence, documents, artifacts, remains, altered ecology, old infrastructure, descendants, and conflicting testimony.

## Content boundary

The simulation produces authoritative state, causes, actors, and consequences. An LLM may produce descriptions, dialogue, names, rumor phrasing, and readable historical summaries, but may not invent facts that contradict authoritative state.
