## Death

Death is somewhat permanent: a player has nine lives. When they're gone,
the character is gone forever and the player has to create a new one. See
[pvp.md](pvp.md) for how these stakes apply (or don't) to player-vs-player
combat specifically.

### Earning extra lives

Players can buy more lives/soul stones with [premium
currency](economy.md#premium-currency-convenience-and-rarity-never-power),
but soul stones are also **rare in-world discoveries** — occasional rare
loot, or rewards from exceptional achievements (a major boss, a major quest
chain). This keeps a soul stone feeling like a genuine treasure rather than
a pure cash-shop item, while premium purchase stays the reliable path for
players who'd rather pay than hunt for one.

### Resurrection

When a player dies, they respawn as a ghost at the nearest graveyard and
have to walk to their body to be resurrected.

- **Other players can help.** A nearby player can resurrect you at your
  body instead of you having to walk there yourself.
- **Free but risky.** Rescuing costs the rescuer no resource, but
  resurrecting takes a few seconds of channeling next to the corpse — the
  rescuer is exposed to whatever killed you in the first place, so helping
  is a real risk decision in a dangerous spot, not a free button. Keeps
  the moment purely social/altruistic (WoW battle-rez-culture energy)
  while still mattering.
- **Gear stays on the corpse, at risk.** Equipped gear/inventory doesn't
  travel with your ghost — it stays on the body until you (or a rescuer)
  reach it, on a decay timer, and is lootable by nearby monsters or
  hostile players in [pvp.md](pvp.md)'s full-loot zones in the meantime.
  Classic corpse-run tension: death costs more than just a tick on the
  nine-lives counter, and it's what gives full-loot PvP zones actual teeth
  rather than just a rule with no consequence attached.
